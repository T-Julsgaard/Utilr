02 what it does

nine hundred thousand names. a few milliseconds.

>all of it

One thread per drive reads every folder. C: with 350,000 entries takes about two seconds from a warm disk.

>found as you type

A search is one pass over the names: 900,000 of them in 5 to 15 ms. Words in any order, "phrases", wildcards, or a folder\ to look in the path.

>the right one first

Exact names and name starts first, what you opened before and your own folders up, machine folders like node_modules well down.

>keeps up

Fixed drives are watched. New, renamed, moved and deleted files show up in about a second. USB drives are read when they appear.

>three looks

A floating bar, a window, or a thin line across the top of the screen. Same shortcuts, same ghost.

>ready at start

The list is cached, so after a restart it's searchable within a tenth of a second while the drives are read again in the background.

04 the details

  • what you can type

    invoice 2026: both words, anywhere in the name. "dm mono": a phrase. *.pdf, img_????.jpg: wildcards, the whole name has to fit. github\ notr: a word with \ is looked for in the whole path.

  • left out

    The recycle bin, System Volume Information, WinSxS, setup leftovers and folders a normal user can't open. Links are listed but not followed, so nothing is indexed twice.

  • drives

    Local and USB drives are in by default; network drives and discs can be turned on. A folder that isn't a drive can be added in the settings file.

  • memory and cost

    A Windows folder's system files are left out unless you want them. With the RAM saver on (utilr's settings), findr's list is put away after 5 minutes unused and loaded again from its cache when you open findr, in a fraction of a second. Watching costs 1–2% of one core while things on disk change, nothing when they don't. Searches are never logged.

  • Windows only

    Drive letters, PowerShell and recursive watching. On macOS and Linux utilr simply leaves findr out.
